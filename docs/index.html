NativeVoice

Dictation that doesn’t stop at English.

Hold a key, speak, let go — the text appears where your cursor is. Dictation on a Mac is excellent in English and patchy to absent in everything else. This is built the other way round.

Built on the ElevenLabs Scribe API. I tried the alternatives on the languages I actually dictate in, and this was the one that got them right — reliably, and accurately enough to trust. It costs cents.

Everything in one menu

There is no settings window to go hunting through. What the app can do, it does from here — and what you see is all of it.

Language, trigger key, recording cap and your vocabulary; sounds, automatic pasting and filler removal; your ElevenLabs usage against its limit; and the log, which explains in plain sentences what the app did and why.

Setting it up

Ten minutes. You bring your own ElevenLabs key: you pay them directly for what you dictate and nothing goes through anyone else. Their free tier is 10,000 credits a month and transcription costs 330 credits a minute — about half an hour of dictation every month for nothing. Check their current pricing before you rely on that.

  1. Drag NativeVoice into Applications and open it. It has no window; look for it in the menu bar.

  2. Make a key at ElevenLabs → Settings → API Keys and copy it at once — they show it only the first time.

  3. Menu → ElevenLabs account → Set API key… → paste → Save.

  1. Say yes when it asks for the microphone, Input Monitoring and Accessibility. It raises the dialogs itself; you do not have to go looking.

  2. Let it restart when it offers. macOS hands Input Monitoring to a freshly started process only, so nothing works until it does.

  3. Hold right ⌘ and speak.
